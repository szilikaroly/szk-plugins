## When to reach for JBI, and when not to

JBI's strength is coverage: it has a checklist for designs that RoB 2, ROBINS-I and QUADAS-2
simply do not address — case reports, case series, prevalence studies, qualitative research.
That is what it is for here.

For designs where a risk-of-bias tool exists, **prefer the risk-of-bias tool**:

| Design | Use | Not |
|---|---|---|
| randomised trial | RoB 2 | JBI RCT checklist |
| non-randomised intervention study | ROBINS-I | JBI quasi-experimental |
| observational study of an exposure | ROBINS-E | JBI cohort |
| diagnostic accuracy | QUADAS-2 | JBI DTA |
| systematic review | AMSTAR 2 / ROBIS | JBI systematic review |

JBI checklists ask whether a study is well conducted; the risk-of-bias tools ask whether *this
result* could be wrong and in which direction. Reviewers increasingly expect the second.

**JBI has no score and no threshold.** The output is a decision — include, exclude, or seek
further information — plus the item answers. Reviews that report "JBI score 7/8" have invented
a metric the instrument does not have.

## Cohort studies — 11 items

**1.1 (cohort) — Were the two groups similar and recruited from the same population?**
**1.2 (cohort) — Were the exposures measured similarly to assign people to both exposed and unexposed groups?**
**1.3 (cohort) — Was the exposure measured in a valid and reliable way?**
**1.4 (cohort) — Were confounding factors identified?**
**1.5 (cohort) — Were strategies to deal with confounding factors stated?**
**1.6 (cohort) — Were the groups/participants free of the outcome at the start of the study?**
**1.7 (cohort) — Were the outcomes measured in a valid and reliable way?**
**1.8 (cohort) — Was the follow-up time reported and sufficient to be long enough for outcomes to occur?**
**1.9 (cohort) — Was follow-up complete, and if not, were the reasons for loss to follow-up described and explored?**
**1.10 (cohort) — Were strategies to address incomplete follow-up utilised?**
**1.11 (cohort) — Was appropriate statistical analysis used?**

## Case-control studies — 10 items

**2.1 (case-control) — Were the groups comparable other than the presence of disease in cases or absence in controls?**
**2.2 (case-control) — Were cases and controls matched appropriately?**
**2.3 (case-control) — Were the same criteria used for identification of cases and controls?**
**2.4 (case-control) — Was exposure measured in a standard, valid and reliable way?**
**2.5 (case-control) — Was exposure measured in the same way for cases and controls?**
**2.6 (case-control) — Were confounding factors identified?**
**2.7 (case-control) — Were strategies to deal with confounding factors stated?**
**2.8 (case-control) — Were outcomes assessed in a standard, valid and reliable way for cases and controls?**
**2.9 (case-control) — Was the exposure period of interest long enough to be meaningful?**
**2.10 (case-control) — Was appropriate statistical analysis used?**

## Analytical cross-sectional studies — 8 items

**3.1 (cross-sectional) — Were the criteria for inclusion in the sample clearly defined?**
**3.2 (cross-sectional) — Were the study subjects and the setting described in detail?**
**3.3 (cross-sectional) — Was the exposure measured in a valid and reliable way?**
**3.4 (cross-sectional) — Were objective, standard criteria used for measurement of the condition?**
**3.5 (cross-sectional) — Were confounding factors identified?**
**3.6 (cross-sectional) — Were strategies to deal with confounding factors stated?**
**3.7 (cross-sectional) — Were the outcomes measured in a valid and reliable way?**
**3.8 (cross-sectional) — Was appropriate statistical analysis used?**

A cross-sectional design cannot establish temporality. Whatever the checklist returns, the
appraisal has to say that — it is the limitation that determines what the study can be cited
for, and it is not an item on the list.

## Case series — 10 items

**4.1 (case-series) — Were there clear criteria for inclusion in the case series?**
**4.2 (case-series) — Was the condition measured in a standard, reliable way for all participants?**
**4.3 (case-series) — Were valid methods used for identification of the condition for all participants?**
**4.4 (case-series) — Did the case series have consecutive inclusion of participants?**
**4.5 (case-series) — Did the case series have complete inclusion of participants?**
**4.6 (case-series) — Was there clear reporting of the demographics of the participants?**
**4.7 (case-series) — Was there clear reporting of clinical information of the participants?**
**4.8 (case-series) — Were the outcomes or follow-up results of cases clearly reported?**
**4.9 (case-series) — Was there clear reporting of the presenting site's/clinic's demographic information?**
**4.10 (case-series) — Was statistical analysis appropriate?**

Items 4.4 and 4.5 are the ones that separate a case series from a collection of memorable
cases, and they are the ones most often answered Unclear.

## Case reports — 8 items

**5.1 (case-report) — Were the patient's demographic characteristics clearly described?**
**5.2 (case-report) — Was the patient's history clearly described and presented as a timeline?**
**5.3 (case-report) — Was the current clinical condition of the patient on presentation clearly described?**
**5.4 (case-report) — Were diagnostic tests or assessment methods and the results clearly described?**
**5.5 (case-report) — Was the intervention or treatment procedure clearly described?**
**5.6 (case-report) — Was the post-intervention clinical condition clearly described?**
**5.7 (case-report) — Were adverse events (harms) or unanticipated events identified and described?**
**5.8 (case-report) — Does the case report provide takeaway lessons?**

Pair this with the **CARE** reporting guideline when the case report is your own: JBI asks
whether it is sound, CARE asks whether it is completely reported, and journals check the second.

## Prevalence studies — 9 items

**6.1 (prevalence) — Was the sample frame appropriate to address the target population?**
**6.2 (prevalence) — Were study participants sampled in an appropriate way?**
**6.3 (prevalence) — Was the sample size adequate?**
**6.4 (prevalence) — Were the study subjects and the setting described in detail?**
**6.5 (prevalence) — Was the data analysis conducted with sufficient coverage of the identified sample?**
**6.6 (prevalence) — Were valid methods used for the identification of the condition?**
**6.7 (prevalence) — Was the condition measured in a standard, reliable way for all participants?**
**6.8 (prevalence) — Was there appropriate statistical analysis?**
**6.9 (prevalence) — Was the response rate adequate, and if not, was the low response rate managed appropriately?**

## Qualitative research — 10 items

**7.1 (qualitative) — Is there congruity between the stated philosophical perspective and the research methodology?**
**7.2 (qualitative) — Is there congruity between the research methodology and the research question or objectives?**
**7.3 (qualitative) — Is there congruity between the research methodology and the methods used to collect data?**
**7.4 (qualitative) — Is there congruity between the research methodology and the representation and analysis of data?**
**7.5 (qualitative) — Is there congruity between the research methodology and the interpretation of results?**
**7.6 (qualitative) — Is there a statement locating the researcher culturally or theoretically?**
**7.7 (qualitative) — Is the influence of the researcher on the research, and vice-versa, addressed?**
**7.8 (qualitative) — Are participants, and their voices, adequately represented?**
**7.9 (qualitative) — Is the research ethical according to current criteria, with evidence of ethical approval?**
**7.10 (qualitative) — Do the conclusions drawn in the research report flow from the analysis or interpretation of the data?**

Do not apply quantitative reasoning here. Sample size, blinding and confounding are not
qualitative concepts, and an appraisal that criticises a qualitative study for lacking them
has appraised the wrong thing. For certainty of qualitative evidence, use **GRADE-CERQual**,
not GRADE.

## Provenance

Aromataris E, Munn Z (eds). *JBI Manual for Evidence Synthesis*, JBI (jbi.global), and the
individual JBI critical appraisal checklists. Item texts are working paraphrases; the
checklists are freely available from JBI and the published wording should be used when an
appraisal appears in a manuscript. The routing advice at the top of this file — prefer the
risk-of-bias tool where one exists — is this file's recommendation, not JBI's.
